## 2. Code

**New module** `choice R pipeline/scripts/00_shared/sensitivity_order_dispersion_robustness.R`
(674 lines). Four blocks:

| Block | What it computes | Output |
|---|---|---|
| A | Correlation of treatment with trial order, testing day, trial within the day, position within tank, holding density, early/late half, tank, motor configuration | `order_orthogonality.csv` |
| B | Each of trial order / testing day / position-within-tank added to the reported model as a **centred** fixed term plus its treatment interaction; treatment estimate with and without; main effect and interaction | `order_position_confound.csv` |
| C | Trial-level SD and CV by treatment, SD ratio, Levene; trial variance component and ICC; whether the **between-trial** variance differs by treatment (1-df LRT against a per-treatment trial variance) and whether the **residual** dispersion differs (`dispformula = ~ treatment`); treatment test refitted under unequal dispersion | `between_trial_dispersion.csv` |
| D | Mann–Whitney on trial-level values; **exact** within-tank permutation (6⁴ = 1296 relabelings, 648 for the monoamines); leave-one-trial-out maximum p | `robustness_rank_perm_loo.csv` |

**Second module** `00_shared/zone_correction_isolation.R` (153 lines) — isolates the
effect of the zone-assignment correction by putting the retained pre-correction
frame-level dataset through the *current* analysis.
Outputs `zone_correction_isolation.csv`, `zone_correction_occupancy_by_config.csv`,
`zone_correction_derived_occupancy.csv`.

**Reproduction gate.** Nothing is written until every one of the 13 outcomes is
refit from source data and its treatment statistic matched against the published
value. All 13 passed exactly (`r26_reproduction_gate.csv`). The zone module has its
own gate: its derivation reproduces the pipeline's own occupancy dataset to 1e-8 and
reproduces the four published ALR statistics, which is what makes the pre-correction
refit interpretable.

**Design note worth keeping.** `phys_trial_id` is **not** chronological — trials 1↔2,
13↔14 and 15↔16 are swapped relative to running order. Trial sequence must be rebuilt
from (date, holding density descending). The one key every dataset shares is
**(tank, fish count)**; the endocrine tables code trial as `"<tank>_<count>"`, and
some endocrine dates are the morning after the trial, so date cannot be part of the key.

## 6. Numbers for the R2.6 rebuttal cell

**1 · Orthogonality is a property of the design.** Treatment correlates **exactly
zero** with trial sequence, testing day, trial within the day, position within
holding tank, holding density, the early/late half, holding tank and motor
configuration. Counterbalanced, not randomised — so this is guaranteed, not lucky.

**2 · The order tests, all 39 of them.** Adding trial order, testing day or
position-within-tank to each reported model left the treatment estimate **unchanged**
for every behavioural outcome (to machine precision — a consequence of exact
orthogonality in a balanced design) and changed it by at most 5.8% for the endocrine
outcomes. No treatment test changed its significance. No added term had a significant
main effect (smallest p = 0.059).

**3 · Three interactions out of 39 reached p < 0.05, against two expected by chance**
— and this is the item that needed the Methods sentence changed:

| Outcome × term | F(1,12) | p | Treatment p without → with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nearest-neighbour distance × trial order | 6.73 | 0.023 | 0.006 → 0.001 |
| Nearest-neighbour distance × testing day | 5.58 | 0.036 | 0.006 → 0.002 |
| Flow–calm crossings × position within tank | 6.63 | 0.024 | < 0.001 → < 0.001 |

In all three the treatment effect became **stronger**, not weaker. They describe a
difference between treatments in how the outcome drifted over the run, not a temporal
trend masquerading as a treatment effect.

**4 · The reviewer is right that the spread differs, and it differs selectively.**
Between-trial SD, exercise choice ÷ control:

| Larger under exercise choice (variance differs, p ≤ 0.002) | | No difference (p ≥ 0.10) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALR(flow) | 5.2× | Flow–calm crossings | 1.1× |
| Inter-individual distance | 5.5× | Longest flow bout | 1.7× |
| Nearest-neighbour distance | 4.0× | Plasma cortisol | 0.6× |
| ALR(high) | 3.5× | Dm DOPAC | 12.0× (n.s.) |
| School area | 3.3× | | |

Not testable: flow bouts per minute, school speed and Dm DA (trial variance estimated
at zero under both treatments); Dm 5-HT (heterogeneous model did not converge).
Reported as such, not as an absence of difference.

Trial ICC 0 – 0.67, highest for exactly the measures that differ (NND 0.51, IID 0.67,
school area 0.55).

**5 · The effects survive.** Under unequal dispersion no significant effect lost its
significance; nine of eleven were more strongly supported. And:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Exact within-tank permutation (1296 relabelings) | 11 of 11 significant, largest p = 0.040 |
| Rank test on trial-level values | 10 of 11 — school area fails (p = 0.279) |
| Leave-one-trial-out | 10 of 11 — Dm 5-HT reaches p = 0.076 on one deletion |

Both exceptions are named in §3.1.4 and interpreted with that caution.

**6 · The zone-assignment correction, isolated.** Frame-level occupancy for the two
motor configurations — which the arena's symmetry requires to agree — disagreed by
**55.4 pp (calm) and 60.2 pp (flow)** before correction, in opposite directions, and
by **1.0 and 2.6 pp** after. Re-running the retained pre-correction dataset through
the *current* code:

| | Pre-correction | Post-correction (reported) |
|---|---|---|
| ALR(flow) | F₁,₁₄ = 0.87, p = 0.367 | F₁,₁₄ = 17.50, p < 0.001 |
| ALR(high) | F₁,₁₄ = 0.16, p = 0.699 | F₁,₁₄ = 7.53, p = 0.016 |
| ALR(medium) | F₁,₁₄ = 0.05, p = 0.828 | F₁,₁₄ = 5.28, p = 0.038 |
| ALR(low) | n.s. | n.s. |

So the occupancy-based conclusions **do** depend on the correction — and the honest
reading is that the fault destroyed the signal rather than that the correction
created one: it was balanced 4/4 across treatment, and it removed the flow sub-zones
for half the trials. This is now stated plainly in §2.5.1 rather than left in the
rebuttal letter.

**7 · The biological reading.** The residual heterogeneity is a result, not noise:
commitment to the flow zone looks like a school-level response that some schools
adopt more completely than others. §3.1.3 says so and points forward to the
leadership and social-facilitation discussion.

**8 · What remains conceded.** Days since transfer, cumulative tank disturbance and
the tank/configuration alignment cannot be separated with 16 schools — Limitations iv).

## 7. Three things that need your decision

1. **The high/medium caveat.** The correction documentation records that the two
   configurations still disagree on the internal split between the high and medium
   sub-zones (49.7% vs 24.1% high) even though they agree on the flow region as a
   whole (65.0% vs 62.4%). I disclosed this in §2.5.1 and in the supplementary note,
   because it is already committed to on paper. It bears on the manuscript's
   graded-preference claim, which rests on ALR(high). Say the word if you would
   rather it came out.

2. **Main-text Table 2 and old Table S9 disagree.** Table 2 reports Dm 5-HT as
   F₁,₁₂ = 5.93, p = 0.032; the supplementary 24-cell table reports the same cell as
   F = 6.11, df 1,34, p = 0.019 — a different model (`lm`, no random effect, vs
   `BC(λ) ~ treatment + (1|trial)`). Table 2's values come from
   `cell_cross_summary_bh.csv` and reproduce exactly; the supplementary table comes
   from a different parameterisation. **Pre-existing, not introduced here, and left
   alone** — but a reader who compares the two will notice.

3. **Figures 4–6** still show means ± SEM with no trial-level points (memo §5.5),
   per your instruction. Reviewer 2 asked to see the dots; they are now quantified in
   Table S10 but not re-plotted.
